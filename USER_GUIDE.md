# Bearing the Color of Scripture — A Quick Guide

## What this is

This app is a companion to reading the Bible, not a replacement for it. It doesn't contain any Bible text at all — you read your own physical Bible, and the app just keeps track of where you are, holds your notes, and helps you build a daily rhythm.

Think of it like a bookmark and a notebook that happen to live on your phone or tablet, rather than a Bible app.

It follows a specific one-year reading plan: five short readings a day (a few Psalms, a chapter of Proverbs, something from the Old Testament, a Gospel chapter, and something from the rest of the New Testament). Over the course of a year, that works out to reading through Psalms and Proverbs twelve times each, the Gospels four times each, and the rest of the Bible once.

Everything you do in the app stays on your own device. There's no account, no login, and nothing is sent anywhere — it works fully offline once it's loaded.

## Getting started

The first time you open it each day, you'll see a short screen with a historic prayer on it — that's just a brief pause before you get going, not a loading screen. Tap **Enter** whenever you're ready; nothing is timed or delayed.

From there you land on **Home**, which just offers four simple destinations: Read, Journal, Prayer, and Library.

## Read — the main screen

This is where you'll spend most of your time. It shows today's five readings, split into a **Morning** and **Evening** group (you can tap between them, and reassign any reading to the other group if you'd rather).

For each reading, you'll see the reference (like "Genesis 1–2") and a **Mark complete** button. On a touchscreen, you can also just swipe left-to-right across a reading to mark it complete — either way does the same thing.

Tap on any reading to open its own notebook page, where you can write freely about it. Markdown formatting works if you want it (**bold**, *italic*, lists, etc.), but plain text is fine too. Anything you write is tied to that specific day's reading — if the plan brings you back to the same passage later in the year, that gets its own separate note, so your notes build up as a real history over time rather than overwriting each other.

**If you miss a reading:** you don't have to do anything right away — it'll just sit there, unmarked, until you get to it. If you'd rather push it to tomorrow instead, there's a **Shift to tomorrow** button, which only moves that one reading (and everything after it in that same category) forward by a day — it never affects your other four readings.

## Journal

Two kinds of freeform writing, separate from your reading notes:

- **Daily Reflection** — one per calendar day
- **Monthly Reflection** — one per month

Both work the same way as reading notes: tap to write, tap away to see it rendered nicely.

## Prayer

A small, built-in collection of historic prayers — a few from the 1662 Book of Common Prayer, one from St. John Chrysostom, one from St. Ephrem the Syrian, and the Jesus Prayer. Nothing modern, nothing under copyright — just short prayers people have been praying for a very long time.

## Library

This is the archive of everything you've written:

- **Scripture Notes** — every reading note you've written, organized by Bible book (Genesis through Revelation)
- **Tags** — if you write `#something` naturally into any note (like `#prayer` or `#question`), it gets indexed here automatically, so you can browse by theme later
- **Progress** — a quiet look at how far you've gotten in each of the five categories, and which books you've touched. No streaks, no scores, nothing gamified — just a record of where you've been
- **Export** — download everything as either a human-readable set of files (a .zip you could open and read directly) or a single backup file. There's also a **Restore** option here if you ever need to load a backup back in — worth knowing that restoring fully replaces whatever's currently on the device, so use it deliberately

## Settings

Tap the small gear icon in the top corner. Two things live there:

- **Theme** — three options (Prayer Book, Candlelight, Minimal), each with a genuinely different look and feel. Try all three.
- **Reading Year** — if you want to start the plan on a different date than whenever you first opened the app, you can change that here.

## One tip: install it like an app

If you're on an iPhone or iPad, open it in Safari, tap the Share button, then **Add to Home Screen**. It'll behave like a real installed app from there — its own icon, no browser address bar, and it still works without an internet connection.

That's really it. There's no tutorial beyond this — the app is meant to stay out of your way and just help you keep a rhythm going.
